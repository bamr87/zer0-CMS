# ADR 0012 — Content lives in lifehacker.dev: five content sites leave this repository for its agencies, and zer0-CMS is the platform that manages them

> lifehacker.dev becomes the content source and zer0-CMS the platform: the five content sites under `sites/` (1987, 2005, drsai, irony-works and wargames) move to lifehacker.dev's `agencies/`, their ledger rows become `specified`, the platform's tests read them from there, and the zer0 demo sites stay.

- **Status:** proposed 2026-11-22; accepted when the owner merges the pull request that carries it. It is numbered 0012 because 0011 is taken by a record on a local branch that has not merged (`feat/design-system`, the design-system tokens), so the two cannot collide whichever lands first.
- **Amends:** [ADR 0002](0002-one-home-cutover.md) — decision 1 ("zer0-CMS is the only place work happens"), decision 4 (the one Pages deploy, which listed `/drsai/` and had `year-of-ai.github.io` build `sites/2005` from here) and decision 5 (DB-first, site by site), **for these five sources only**. [ADR 0006](0006-one-python-platform.md) — decision 10 (a source directory goes only when its capabilities have moved; [ADR 0008](0008-retire-the-rails-cms.md) added `specified` for a source the owner deleted, and this record adds a source the owner moved) and decision 13 (a site's files are generated from the database and committed so that git keeps the history: for these five, the git that keeps it is lifehacker.dev's), **for these five sources only**. Every other decision of both records stands, and for every other source both stand unchanged.
- **Inputs:** the owner's direction of 2026-11-22, in their words: "review the site directory in zer0-cms, and consolidate/migrate all the content into lifehacker.dev as different sub-agencies. the goal is to transform lifehacker as the content source, and zer0-CMS is the platform. year-of-ai and ai-world-view should also be under lifehacker. the zer0 demosites should stay in zer0-cms."
- **Where:** `sites/1987`, `sites/2005`, `sites/drsai`, `sites/irony-works` and `sites/wargames` (removed); their projects in `workspace.yml`; the workflows `drsai.yml` and `irony-works.yml` (deleted), `sites.yml`, `pages.yml` and `content.yml`; the `site-1987`, `site-2005`, `site-drsai`, `site-irony-works` and `site-wargames` rows of [`../plan/capabilities.yml`](../plan/capabilities.yml); [`../plan/agencies-spec.md`](../plan/agencies-spec.md); `content/tests/agencies.py` and the tests that read through it.

## Context

[ADR 0002](0002-one-home-cutover.md) made this repository the only home of everything merged into it, and [ADR 0006](0006-one-python-platform.md) absorbs every merged repository with its content and capabilities, holds content in the database and generates each site's files. The nine sites under `sites/` were treated alike, and they are two different things. Four are the zer0 stack's own demonstration sites — zer0-pages, zer0-pages-remote and the two installer demos — which exist to exercise the platform and the Jekyll-theme option. Five are bodies of content with a subject, a readership and tooling of their own: a knowledge base that grows itself, another from the year-of-ai lineage, a picture-book site with a story engine, an encyclopedia with a germination engine, and a study site re-synced from an upstream.

Keeping those five here made the platform's repository the place their content is written, and made every content change a change to the platform. The owner separates the two roles: one repository is the content source, one is the platform. The platform already has that relationship with other site repositories — a checkout under `SITES_DIR` that it registers, syncs, writes back under a stale-write guard and checks with the doctor (PLAN G4) — so the five become what those are: external sites.

Two facts about the state on 2026-11-22 shaped the record. The original repositories of four of the five no longer exist (the GitHub API answers 404 for `bamr87/1987`, `bamr87/drsai`, `bamr87/irony-works` and `bamr87/wargames`; `year-of-ai/2005` is archived and still serves its last build), so this repository held the only copy of those trees. And this repository's Pages deploy was never switched on (no Pages site, no `PAGES_DEPLOY_ENABLED` variable), so the sites it routed were built and checked on every pull request and served from nowhere.

The owner's direction also places year-of-ai and ai-world-view under lifehacker.dev. Neither is held here beyond `sites/2005`, so that part is lifehacker.dev's own record.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Mechanism that enforces it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **lifehacker.dev is the content source; zer0-CMS is the platform.** A content site's file tree lives in `bamr87/lifehacker.dev`, each a self-contained tree under `agencies/`, registered in that repository's `_data/agencies.yml`. zer0-CMS imports, round-trips, renders and manages those trees as external sites, reached through `SITES_DIR` like any other site repository, and no longer holds them in its monorepo. | `bin/zer0 check` (`containers_registered`): a directory under `sites/` that `workspace.yml` does not register fails, and the five are no longer projects. `tools/check_absorption.py`: their `theme_ratchet:` prefixes are gone, so a configuration file naming the theme under `sites/2005/`, `sites/drsai/`, `sites/irony-works/` or `sites/wargames/` is new theme dependency outside the ratchet and fails. Beyond that, review: this record and the ledger say where each tree belongs. |
| 2 | **What moves, and where.** Whole, from this repository at `9b68af57` — content, site configuration and each site's own tooling: `sites/1987` → `agencies/1987`; `sites/2005` → `agencies/year-of-ai/2005`; `sites/drsai` → `agencies/drsai`; `sites/irony-works` → `agencies/irony-works`; `sites/wargames` → `agencies/wargames`. | The import and its record are lifehacker.dev's (`_data/agencies.yml`: source commit, file count and digest per tree). Here the trees are removed, and [`agencies-spec.md`](../plan/agencies-spec.md) § Where each source lives keeps the mapping; they stay recoverable from this repository's history (`git archive 9b68af57 sites/<name>`). |
| 3 | **What stays.** `sites/zer0-demo-cookbook`, `sites/zer0-demo-garden`, `sites/zer0-pages` and `sites/zer0-pages-remote`, and everything under `apps/` and `packages/`, untouched. ADR 0002 and ADR 0006 hold for them as before. | Their `workspace.yml` projects, workflows and ledger rows are unchanged; `bin/zer0 check` and `tools/workspace/tests/test_real_manifest.py`. |
| 4 | **The five sources' open rows are `specified`.** Each of the 14 rows names this decision in `decided_by` and its section of [`agencies-spec.md`](../plan/agencies-spec.md) in `spec`: what the capability did here, where its source lives now, where the platform stands and what it still owes. Each keeps its `target`: the platform owes it. `specified` therefore widens from "the owner deleted the source" (ADR 0008) to "the owner took the source out of this repository — deleted it, or moved it to another one". A source's `path:` keeps naming the directory this repository held; it never points at the new home, because its absence is what the deletion rule tests. | `tools/check_absorption.py`, its code unchanged: a `specified` row needs `decided_by`, a spec file that exists and a target that is still a platform capability; a source whose `path` is gone with a row `missing` or `partial` fails. `tools/tests/test_check_absorption.py`. |

They read the trees from an agencies directory — `ZER0_AGENCIES_DIR`, else a sibling checkout `../lifehacker.dev/agencies` — resolved in one place. `content.yml` checks `agencies/` out of `bamr87/lifehacker.dev` (a public repository, at its default branch) in both of its jobs. A tree that is absent skips its cases with a reason that names the variable. | `content/tests/agencies.py`; `content/tests/importers/test_jekyll.py::test_the_real_sites_round_trip_byte_for_byte`, `content/tests/importers/test_blocks.py`, `content/tests/distribution/test_lane.py::test_every_site_gets_the_urls_the_ruby_lane_gave_it` and `content/tests/books/test_abc.py`; `content.yml`, which prints the trees it found. |
| 6 | **The recorded builds stay, as the parity record.** `web/site/fixtures/{2005,drsai,irony-works,wargames}/` are kept unedited. Nothing here rebuilds those sites, so nothing compares against them; whoever publishes one next reproduces its recording or re-records it and says why. | `AGENTS.md` (a recording is re-recorded with `tools/site_manifest.py`, never edited); `tools/site_manifest.py compare`. |
| 7 | **The workspace and CI follow.** The five projects leave `workspace.yml`; `drsai.yml` and `irony-works.yml` are deleted; `sites.yml` builds the three sites that remain in it; `pages.yml`, whose plan is `workspace.yml`'s routes, no longer builds `/drsai/` and `/wargames/`. ## Consequences

What it costs, plainly:

- **This repository's CI no longer builds those five sites.** Gone with them: their builds through the Jekyll-theme option; the comparison of each build with its recording (`sites.yml` for 2005, `irony-works.yml`, `pages.yml` for drsai and wargames); drsai's story-engine lint and tests on three Pythons and its ABC drift check. None of that runs anywhere until lifehacker.dev, or the platform over an external checkout, runs it.
- **`/drsai/` and `/wargames/` leave this repository's Pages plan.** No public URL changes, because the deploy never ran.
- **The round-trip corpus for those sites lives in another repository.** It floats on lifehacker.dev's default branch: a change there can turn `content.yml` red here with no change here, and nothing in this repository's history says which bytes a past run read. Where a tree is absent — before the import lands, or after lifehacker.dev renames an agency — its cases skip rather than fail, visible as a notice and in pytest's count. And the suite now runs one script from that branch, drsai's `tools/import_abc_book.py`, as the oracle of the ABC export: code this repository's review no longer sees, run in a job that holds a read-only token.
- **Publishing the agencies from lifehacker.dev is not built yet, and is the owner's next decision.** Until it is made, drsai, irony-works, wargames and 1987 have no public site at all, and 2005 is served, frozen, by the archived `year-of-ai/2005`.
- **The Jekyll-theme option cannot build an external checkout.** It builds a project `workspace.yml` names from the files this repository's git tracks. Rendering an agency through the platform is owed ([`agencies-spec.md`](../plan/agencies-spec.md)).
- **The moved trees still name commands that resolved into this repository** (`python3 ../../tools/jekyll_theme.py build drsai`). They were moved unedited; correcting them belongs where they live.
- **Two recordings still describe the five sites as they were here**: the workspace tool's golden recordings, frozen at commit `40c904b9`, and the distribution lane's recording of the Ruby lane. Both are records of a past tree and are unchanged.
- **ADR 0002's cutover ends for these five.** C1, C4, C6 and C7 have nothing left to do for them here, and their rows leave [`../MONOREPO.md`](../MONOREPO.md) § What still depends on the original repositories.

## Open — the owner's decisions

1. **Where the agencies publish, and at which URLs** — lifehacker.dev's own Pages, the platform's Jekyll-theme option over an external checkout, or the React renderer — and whether drsai, irony-works and wargames return at the URLs their recordings hold. lifehacker.dev has since answered for one of them: on 2026-11-23 it published Wargames as a collection of its own site under `/wargames/` (its ADR 0003), so `agencies/wargames` is a back office with no pages, and the Wargames cases left this suite ([`agencies-spec.md`](../plan/agencies-spec.md#site-wargames)).
2. **Pin or float.** `content.yml` reads lifehacker.dev's default branch. Pinning a commit (as the theme's docs fixture is pinned at a tag) would make a run reproducible, a corpus change deliberate and the one script the suite runs from there a reviewed one, at the price of a bump per change.
3. **Skip or fail.** Once the import has landed, a missing tree could fail the suite instead of skipping.
4. **Discovery depth.** The platform discovers Jekyll roots one or two levels under `SITES_DIR`. From a lifehacker.dev checkout `agencies/year-of-ai/2005` is three levels down: it registers by path and is not discovered ([`agencies-spec.md`](../plan/agencies-spec.md) § How the platform reaches an agency today).
5. **1987's shape.** It has no `_config.yml`, so the platform can neither register nor import it.
