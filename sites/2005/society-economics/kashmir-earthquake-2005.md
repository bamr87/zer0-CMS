# 2005 Kashmir Earthquake

**Category**: Society & Economics
**Key figures**: Pervez Musharraf (President of Pakistan)

## Summary

At 08:50 local time on October 8, 2005, a moment-magnitude 7.6 earthquake struck the mountainous border region of South Asia, with its epicentre roughly 19 kilometres northeast of Muzaffarabad, the capital of Pakistan-administered Kashmir, and about 90 kilometres north-northeast of Islamabad. The rupture occurred along a thrust fault in the seismically active zone where the Indian and Eurasian plates collide to form the Himalayas.

The shock devastated northern Pakistan and Pakistan-administered Kashmir and was felt across northern India and into Afghanistan. The Pakistani government's official death toll stood at approximately 87,350, with some estimates placing the total above 100,000; around 138,000 people were injured and more than 3.5 million were left homeless. The collapse of poorly constructed buildings caused most casualties, and an estimated 19,000 of the dead were children killed when their schools fell during morning classes.

Relief efforts were hampered by the rugged Himalayan terrain, blocked roads, and the approach of a severe winter that threatened survivors in high-altitude villages. A large international response delivered helicopters, field hospitals, tents, and supplies, and Pakistan and India briefly opened crossing points along the Line of Control to permit aid and family reunification.

## Significance

The 2005 Kashmir earthquake was among the deadliest natural disasters in South Asian history and exposed the vulnerability of unreinforced masonry construction across the region. The catastrophe prompted reforms in disaster preparedness, including the establishment of Pakistan's National Disaster Management Authority in 2007, and spurred reconstruction programmes emphasising earthquake-resistant building standards. The cross-border relief cooperation, though limited, marked a rare moment of humanitarian coordination between Pakistan and India over the disputed Kashmir region.
