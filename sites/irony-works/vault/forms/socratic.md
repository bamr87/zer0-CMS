# Socratic Irony

**Definition.** Feigned ignorance deployed as an instrument — the questioner plays the fool so the expert can discover he is one.

**Mechanism.** The Greek *eirōneia* originally meant something close to sly dissembling; [[socrates]] elevated it into method. The expectation is set by the pose ("I know nothing; teach me"); the reversal arrives when the expert's answers, faithfully followed, dismantle themselves. The audience of this irony is the interlocutor — eventually.

**Commonly confused with.** Sealioning and bad-faith questioning. The test is the direction of the harvest: Socratic irony aims to leave the interlocutor *knowing more* (starting with the size of what they don't). Its bad-faith twin aims only to exhaust.

## Exemplars
- [[socrates]] — the patent holder, and the form's first casualty
- [[a-modest-proposal]] — a cousin: the feigned *sincerity* rather than feigned ignorance
