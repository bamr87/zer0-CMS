# Historical Irony

**Definition.** Irony that only hindsight can see: the confident past judged by the outcome it could not imagine.

**Mechanism.** The expectation was sincerely held by its era — the invention hailed as salvation, the "war to end all wars," the memo declining the patent. The reversal is supplied by time, which makes historians the form's only reliable audience. It is [[dramatic]] irony at civilizational scale: we are the audience that knows the ending; the past is the character on stage.

**Commonly confused with.** Simple error. Being wrong is not ironic; being wrong *in a way that inverts the confident claim* is. The prediction "this will never work" failing is error; the safety device causing the accident is irony.

**Handling note.** This form is the vault's chief `veracity` risk — hindsight loves to sharpen quotes. See [[nobels-obituary]] for the flagging discipline.
