# Antinomy

**Definition.** A paradox that derives a contradiction by accepted reasoning from accepted premises — so that something accepted has to go, and nothing in the argument says what.

**Mechanism.** Quine's third way, and the only one that changes the rules. A veridical paradox costs an intuition and a falsidical one costs a fallacy; an antinomy costs a *principle*, because every premise and every step was one the field had already signed. Russell's set forced naive comprehension out of set theory; the Liar forced truth out of its own language — into Tarski's hierarchy, or into gaps, or into gluts. The resolution of an antinomy is therefore a revision, and the revision is usually contested for generations, which is why most antinomies in this vault carry `standing: contested`. Quine's remark that one man's antinomy is another man's falsidical paradox, give or take a couple of thousand years, is the vault's reason for recording `kind` as a judgment rather than a fact: an antinomy is a falsidical paradox whose fallacy has not been named yet, or a veridical one whose conclusion nobody is yet prepared to accept. In this vault the `collision` field of an antinomy names *logic itself* — the conclusion is a contradiction, or a sentence that is true if and only if it is false.

**Commonly confused with.** A bare contradiction, and a dilemma. "P and not-P" asserted outright has no plausible premises and is [[the-merely-puzzling|not even wrong]]; a hard choice between two goods — the trolley — is a conflict of values, not of truths, and reasoning does not collapse in it, only comfort. The antinomy's signature is that the reader accepts every step and rejects the destination.

## Exemplars
- [[the-liar]] — the founding antinomy; its folk version fails the gate that bears its name
- [[the-heap]] — vagueness as antinomy, or as a false premise, depending on whom you ask
- [[russells-paradox]] — the antinomy that got resolved, at the price of a concept
- [[newcombs-problem]] — an antinomy of decision: two principles of choice in open conflict
