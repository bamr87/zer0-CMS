# Falsidical Paradox

**Definition.** A paradox whose conclusion is false: somewhere in the premises or the inference a fallacy is hiding, and the paradox lasts exactly as long as the hiding does.

**Mechanism.** Quine's second way. The argument looks sound and is not; the work of the paradox is locating the fault, and the *quality* of the paradox is how well the fault hides. Zeno's runner — Quine's own example — hid its fault, that infinitely many intervals must sum to an infinite duration, for roughly two millennia, until convergent series made the sum finite. A schoolbook proof that one equals two hides its division by zero for about a minute. Both are falsidical; only one is worth an entry. In this vault the `collision` field of a falsidical paradox names a *fact* the conclusion contradicts, and the `fault` field names the premise or step that gives way — or, while the search is on, `unlocated`. Hypothetical paradoxes usually live here: a paradox that rides on a counterfactual is often resolved by locating the fault in the hypothesis itself, and the entry's `hypothesis` field keeps that move visible.

**Commonly confused with.** A transparent fallacy, and an equivocation. The gate's test is time: a fault a competent reader finds in seconds is a puzzle, and a conclusion that dissolves the moment one word is disambiguated — nothing is better than eternal happiness; a ham sandwich is better than nothing — is an [[the-merely-puzzling|equivocation]]. A falsidical paradox has to have fooled someone who was paying attention.
