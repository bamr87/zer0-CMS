# Oscar Wilde

**The instrument.** [[verbal]] irony as evening wear. Wilde's epigrams work by warm inversion — take the era's pieties, reverse the polarity, deliver with perfect manners — and their durability comes from being funnier *and* truer than the originals.

**The recoil.** In February 1895 *The Importance of Being Earnest* opened: a flawless comedy about the gap between name and nature, by a man living one. Weeks later the Marquess of Queensberry left a card accusing him of it. Wilde sued for libel — and since truth was a defense, his suit obliged the defense to prove the accusation. It could. The libel case collapsed, the evidence flowed directly into his criminal prosecution, and the man who made a career of the unprovable double meaning was imprisoned by the burden of proof he himself had summoned. The [[cosmic]] timing — masterpiece and catastrophe in the same season, on the same theme — is the kind of arrangement fate reserves for its rivals.
