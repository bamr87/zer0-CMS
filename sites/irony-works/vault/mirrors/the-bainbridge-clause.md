# The Bainbridge Clause

[[ironies-of-automation|Bainbridge's 1983 findings]], executed against this repository: the germination engine handles scouting, drafting, scoring, and linking — everything except deciding what deserves canon. The human role is therefore, by construction, the residue of the impossible task. And the trap has a second jaw: as the engine's hit rate rises, merges become routine, review attention thins, and the curator's calibration — the felt sense of what clears the gate — decays from lack of exercise. The system is most dangerous at its best.

The vault's countermeasures are structural, not aspirational: the gate publishes its *scores and reasoning*, not just verdicts, so review stays an act of checking rather than trusting; the compost keeps rejections visible so the curator's taste trains on negatives too; and the guardrails ensure the one unautomatable decision cannot be delegated even by accident. Whether countermeasures beat clause is an open experiment. This entry is the lab notebook's first page.
