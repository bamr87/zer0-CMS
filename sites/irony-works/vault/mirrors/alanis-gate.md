This vault's quality control is named for the most famous failure of the genre. The 1996 single lists misfortunes and calls them ironic; the objection has been raised so often it has become a genre of its own, and the objectors are right about every verse. They are wrong about the song. A composition titled "Ironic" whose contents are not ironic performs the reversal it names — the expectation is stated in the title, the reversal is delivered by the tracklist, and the commentary is unavoidable. The song passes. Its scenarios do not. The gate is built on exactly that distinction.

Five axes, zero to two each, threshold seven. **Reversal**: is a genuine expectation genuinely inverted, or did something merely go badly? **Commentary**: does the reversal *say something* about the expectation — the substitution test being whether "ironically" can be swapped for "unfortunately" with nothing lost? **Inevitability**: surprise with hindsight-coherence, since the best ironies look composed after the fact. **Specificity**: names, dates, verifiability, checked against the entry's own `veracity` claim. **Non-substitutability**: it must not reduce to a neighbour — bad luck, coincidence, hypocrisy, or plain tragedy, each of which wears irony's coat and none of which is irony.

Failures are not deleted. They go to the [[the-merely-unfortunate|compost ledger]] with a verdict, because the negative space of a definition teaches as much as the definition, and because history occasionally files an appeal. The gate's inaugural session composted the song's entire tracklist — rain, spoons, the no-smoking sign — while the song itself stands here in canon. That is the rubric working, and it is also, unavoidably, the joke: a machine that rejects Alanis Morissette's examples while being named after her, and that owes its own standard to the record it keeps overruling.
