# Nobel's Obituary

The story as told: when Alfred's brother Ludvig died in 1888, a French paper confused the brothers and published Alfred's obituary — an unflattering one, remembered as calling him a dealer in death who grew rich finding faster ways to kill. Alfred, granted the rarest of gifts — a preview of his legacy with time to revise — rewrote his will in 1895 and endowed the prizes that made his surname mean the opposite of dynamite.

**Why this entry is filed `legend`.** The will, the prizes, and the reputational inversion are documented fact. The triggering clipping is not: historians and archivists have had persistent trouble producing the actual 1888 obituary, and the tale has the too-perfect symmetry that should raise any curator's hand toward the [[the-merely-unfortunate|compost lever]]. It stays in canon as a *documented legend* — an irony about a man whose motivating irony may itself be manufactured, which is arguably a third layer. This entry is the vault's standing exhibit for the `veracity` field: the better the story, the harder it must be checked.
