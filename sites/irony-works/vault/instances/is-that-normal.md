# Is That Normal?

The question gets asked in a particular tone — in elevators, in group chats, after each fresh headline — and its grammar does the confessing. Present tense: *is*, not *was*. The demonstrative *that*: the event is still close enough to point at. And *normal*, a noun the asker already suspects has absorbed the thing in question, because nobody asks whether the extraordinary is normal; the question exists for events midway through their paperwork. It is not a request for information. It is the sound an expectation makes on the way out.

The mechanism is the gate's own first axis, run in reverse. Irony burns held expectation for fuel, so irony at sufficient frequency is self-extinguishing: each reversal spends a little of the surprise the next one needs, and an age of ambient, colossal ironies does not feel like an age of irony — it feels like weather. Language records the spend-down. Words bleach under load, as the grammaticalization literature has said since 1891, and the worn coins are the ones you would expect: *awesome*, *unprecedented*, *literally* — now certified by the dictionaries to intensify its own opposite. *Ironic* bleached early and in public; the most widely heard use of the word in the language is a tracklist of things that aren't ([[alanis-gate]]). And *the new normal* is the white flag itself, a phrase that performs the flattening it reports and completes a little more of it per use.

The founder of this vault, commissioning the entry, described "epic ironies that defy the linguistic limits of reality," and the phrase identifies the bind precisely: the ironies now outrun the lexicon. The taxonomy holds [[dramatic]], [[situational]], [[cosmic]]; it has no stable term for reversal at civilizational scale, and it cannot get one, because any coined term is issued into exactly the environment that bleaches terms. Naming the condition normalizes it a little further — an observer effect, for rhetoric. This vault's own lifecycle admits as much: entry status runs seed, sapling, canon, and ends at *petrified*, which is the vault planning for the day its best exhibits read as furniture — dead ironies, in the sense linguists mean when they say dead metaphor.

Hence the `veracity` above: *contested*, applied by the entry to itself. "Our ironies are uniquely epic" is precisely the claim every age files on its own behalf, and a claim that flatters the claimant gets flagged — what is attested is smaller and harder. The words are worn. The question is being asked. The answer it expects has changed. *Is that normal?* No — "normal" is just the name for whatever stopped being asked about, and the gate downstairs exists to keep one word, at least, from bleaching all the way through.
