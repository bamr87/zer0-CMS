# The Merely Puzzling
*Candidates that failed the [[epimenides-gate|Epimenides Gate]]. Kept, because the negative space of a definition is instructive — and because one man's antinomy is another man's falsidical paradox, give or take a couple of thousand years.*

Verdicts: `MERELY-SURPRISING` (a finding with a headline; nothing derived) · `EQUIVOCATION` (dissolves when one word is disambiguated) · `TRANSPARENT-FALLACY` (the trick is visible in seconds) · `DILEMMA` (a conflict of values, not of truths) · `ANOMALY` (a fact against a theory, no argument between them) · `NOT-EVEN-WRONG` (no premise can be stated)

| Date | Candidate | Score | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-09 | "All Cretans are liars," said by a Cretan (Epimenides) | 5/10 | TRANSPARENT-FALLACY | The negation of "every Cretan always lies" is "some Cretan sometimes tells the truth" — a consistent state of affairs in which Epimenides is lying as usual. The sentence is merely false. Its strengthened descendant is canon: [[the-liar]]. The gate is named for this row. |
| 2026-09-09 | Nothing is better than eternal happiness; a ham sandwich is better than nothing; therefore a ham sandwich is better than eternal happiness | 3/10 | EQUIVOCATION | "Nothing" is a quantifier in the first premise and a noun in the second. Disambiguate it and the syllogism has no middle term. |
| 2026-09-09 | The French paradox (saturated fat, low coronary mortality) | 2/10 | ANOMALY | An observation a theory did not predict. No premises, no inference, no conclusion anyone refuses — only a dataset and a headline. Appeal noted: state the diet-heart hypothesis as premises and the anomaly becomes a falsidical candidate about it. |
| 2026-09-09 | The paradox of choice | 2/10 | MERELY-SURPRISING | More options, less satisfaction, is a finding — contested since — with the word "paradox" in the title. Nothing is derived; "surprisingly" survives the substitution test. |
| 2026-09-09 | The trolley problem | 3/10 | DILEMMA | A hard choice between two goods. Reasoning does not collapse in it, only comfort; there is no conclusion the reader refuses, only one the reader regrets. |
| 2026-09-09 | "Jumbo shrimp," and oxymorons generally | 0/10 | NOT-EVEN-WRONG | A phrase, not an argument. No premise can be stated, so nothing can collide. |

*Note the pattern: the gate's inaugural composting opens with the sentence it is named for. The sentence's descendant is canon — see [[the-liar]] and [[antinomy]].*
