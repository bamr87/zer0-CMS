Summer arrived hot and golden, and it was the day of the Great Watermelon Race! Everyone would roll a giant watermelon down Main Street using only their nose.

But when the sheriff lined up the melons that morning, he gasped. "Hold everything! Every melon has a bite out of it — but only the *ripest* ones!"

It was true. The plain melons sat untouched. But each of the sweetest, reddest, most perfect watermelons had one tidy little *chomp* taken right out of it.

"Now that's a strange clue," said Deputy Dodge. "Our nibbler doesn't just grab any old melon. It picks the best ones every time."

"So it's a melon *expert,*" said Reena. "Someone who knows how to find the ripe ones. But who?"

They hunted for tracks. There were no hops like a rabbit, no paw prints like a coyote. Instead, in the dust, they found a strange wide groove — a long, smooth trail, as if something heavy had dragged itself slowly along.

"Whatever it is," said Reena, puzzled, "it moves *slowly.* Very, very slowly."

{% include components/book-plate.html src="/assets/images/books/dodge-and-reena/06-june-melon-clues.jpg" alt="The sheriff points at the row of nibbled watermelons while Reena traces the strange slow drag-track in the dust" %}

They followed the slow groove behind the melon wagon, around a barrel, under a shady bush... and there, taking the tiniest, most thoughtful bite out of the ripest melon of all, was an old desert tortoise.

*Chomp.* He chewed. He swallowed. He looked up at them with calm, sleepy eyes, in absolutely no hurry at all.

{% include components/book-plate.html src="/assets/images/books/dodge-and-reena/06-june-tortoise-culprit.jpg" alt="The old desert tortoise calmly munches the ripest watermelon under the race banner as the friends watch, amazed" %}

"A tortoise!" laughed Dodge. "The slowest critter in the whole desert is our melon thief!"

"And the smartest melon-picker, too," said Reena. "He can tell the ripe ones by smell. Maybe he can teach *us.*"

So the friends gave the wise old tortoise a melon of his very own, and watched which one he chose — the ripest, juiciest melon in the patch. From then on, whenever the town wanted to know if a melon was ready, they simply asked the tortoise.

Then — *"Ready... set... GO!"* — the race was on! Boop, boop, boop went Dodge's nose. Boop, boop, boop went Reena's. They rolled neck and neck and crossed the line at the very same moment — THUD!

"A tie!" cried the sheriff.

Dodge and Reena just laughed and split the biggest, ripest slice — picked, of course, by the slowest suspect in Dusty Gulch.
