yield:
  amount: 4
  unit: servings
  singular: serving

times:
  prep: 15
  cook: 80

equipment:
  - Wide casserole with a lid

ingredients:
  - group: The braise
    items:
      - item: bone-in chicken thighs, skin on
        qty: 8
      - item: olive oil
        qty: 2
        unit: tbsp
      - item: onions
        singular: onion
        qty: 2
        prep: sliced
      - item: garlic cloves
        singular: garlic clove
        qty: 4
        prep: sliced
      - item: ground cumin
        qty: 1
        unit: tsp
      - item: ground ginger
        qty: 1
        unit: tsp
      - item: saffron threads
        qty: 1
        unit: pinch
        note: Optional, and genuinely optional.
      - item: chicken stock
        qty: 400
        unit: ml
  - group: To finish
    items:
      - item: preserved lemons
        singular: preserved lemon
        qty: 2
        prep: flesh discarded, skin sliced thin
      - item: green olives
        qty: 150
        unit: g
        prep: pitted
      - item: coriander
        qty: 0.5
        unit: cup
        prep: chopped

steps:
  - title: Colour the skin
    text: >-
      Salt the thighs and lay them skin-down in a dry, wide pan over medium heat.
      Do not move them. Let the fat render and the skin go deep gold — eight to
      ten minutes. Turn once, then lift them out.
    time: 12
  - title: Soften and spice
    text: >-
      Pour off most of the fat. Cook the onions in what is left until soft and
      golden, then add garlic, cumin, ginger and saffron for a minute.
    time: 15
  - title: Braise
    text: >-
      Return the thighs skin-up, add the stock so it comes halfway up but leaves
      the skin proud, and simmer covered at a low heat until the meat pulls from
      the bone.
    time: 45
  - title: Sharpen
    text: >-
      Add the preserved lemon and olives, cook uncovered for ten minutes to
      reduce, and finish with coriander. Taste before salting — the lemons and
      olives bring plenty.
    time: 12

notes:
  - Keeping the skin above the liquid is the whole trick; submerged skin goes flabby and there is no fixing it.
  - Preserved lemon is worth buying rather than substituting. Fresh lemon is a different, sharper thing.
---

The first time I made this I salted it like a normal braise and it was inedible. Preserved lemons and olives are both salt-delivery systems; the stock and the seasoning have to make room for them.
