# Style guide — The Cost Line

Every AI task on this book receives this guide in its brief. Keep it short and true.

## Voice

- Plain, direct sentences. Short paragraphs. Dark comedy from precision, not from jokes.
- Third person limited on Elias, past tense. We know what he notices; he notices numbers, timestamps and what people do not say.
- The narration never uses corporate language sincerely. Words like "alignment", "synergy", "leverage", "unlock" and "imperative" appear only in quotes, on slides, or in a character's mouth, and the narration treats them as artefacts.
- The disaster outside is reported flatly, like weather. The horror is that the inside does not react.
- Finance is rendered accurately and simply. A reader who has never closed a quarter should follow every beat.

## Comedy

- The funniest line is usually the most literal description of what happened.
- Let euphemisms stand next to what they mean, without comment.
- No winking at the reader. No one is a cartoon.

## Heart

- June is never a device. Scenes with her are specific: the cup, the song, the time on the clock.
- Fatherhood scenes are written in the same plain voice; no sentimental swell.

## Don'ts

- No real companies, people, places or dates. No money figures for the protagonist's exit.
- No buzzwords in narration; no "little did he know"; no outline references in prose.
