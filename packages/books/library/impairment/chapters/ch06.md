- Reveal the environmental fallout from the protagonist's company's exported equipment.
- Deepen the protagonist's guilt and sense of responsibility.
- Introduce the old colleague as a source of truth and local perspective.
- Connect personal and global consequences of corporate actions.
- Tie the environmental crisis to the earlier international deal and layoffs, showing the ripple effect from the boardroom to the village.

**Setting:**

- Rural village in the protagonist's country of origin.
- Polluted river, dying crops, anxious community.
- Contrast between natural beauty and industrial damage.
- Echoes of the protagonist's memories from earlier chapters, now tainted by the consequences of the company's actions.

**Main Character(s):**

- Protagonist: recently laid off, searching for answers, haunted by his role.
- Old Colleague: local engineer, witness to the environmental crisis, voice of the community.

**Plot Progression:**

1. Protagonist receives a message from his old colleague about a crisis back home, just as he is reeling from his layoff (see Ch. 5).
2. He travels (physically or virtually) to the village, witnessing the devastation and recalling the pride and unease he felt during the original deal (Ch. 2).
3. The old colleague explains the link between the exported equipment and the pollution, referencing the rushed specs and vague requirements from the boardroom (Ch. 2, Ch. 3).
4. Protagonist sees the human cost: sick children, ruined fields, fear in the villagers—mirroring the loss and dislocation he and his colleagues felt after the layoffs (Ch. 4, Ch. 5).

6. As Kareem surveys the devastation, he recalls the pride he once felt in facilitating the deal, now twisted into guilt. He is forced to confront his dual identity—American engineer and son of this wounded land—feeling complicit in both worlds.
7. The chapter ends with the protagonist overwhelmed by guilt and a sense of complicity, setting up his resolve to investigate further (leading into Ch. 7).

**Themes Introduced:**

- Environmental Injustice

- The interconnectedness of personal, corporate, and global consequences

### Chapter Body

The message arrived at dawn, its subject line stark: "You need to see this." The protagonist hesitated before opening it, already suspecting what waited inside. A single photograph—children standing ankle-deep in a river turned the color of rust, their faces pinched with confusion and fear. In the background, the familiar silhouette of the equipment he once helped design, now looming like a monument to regret.

He called his old colleague, the engineer who had stayed behind when he left for America. The voice on the other end was tired, brittle. "It started after the machines arrived. The water changed. The crops failed. People are getting sick."

The protagonist’s mind raced through technical specs, safety protocols, the promises made in glossy brochures. None of it mattered now. He booked a flight, but the journey was more than miles—it was a return to a place he’d tried to outgrow, and a reckoning with the consequences of his ambition. As the plane descended, he remembered the boardroom’s cold logic (Ch. 3), the pride he’d felt when the deal was announced (Ch. 2), and the hollow comfort of the layoff letter (Ch. 5).

The village was smaller than he remembered, the fields patchy and brown. The river, once the lifeblood of the community, now stank of chemicals. He walked with his old colleague along the bank, listening to stories of livestock dying, children coughing through the night, families forced to buy bottled water they could barely afford. The company’s logo was still visible on the side of the equipment, faded but unmistakable—a brand on the landscape, a scar on his conscience.

"They said it would bring jobs," the old colleague said, voice flat. "But all it brought was this."

The protagonist watched a group of children skipping stones, their laughter thin and uncertain. He felt the weight of every decision, every shortcut, every time he’d told himself it was just business. The layoffs back in America had felt like the end of an era (Ch. 4, Ch. 5), but here, the consequences were measured in poisoned water and lost futures.

He wanted to apologize, to explain, to fix it. But the words caught in his throat. The damage was done, and he was part of it. As dusk fell, he resolved to find out how this had happened—to trace the decisions from the boardroom to the riverbank, and to hold someone, perhaps even himself, accountable (setting up his investigation in Ch. 7).
