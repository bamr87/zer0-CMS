# Chapter Body

The invitation arrived on thick, cream-colored stock, embossed with the company’s crest. It was the kind of thing Kareem’s father would have framed, a symbol of arrival in a world that once seemed unreachable. He ran his thumb over the raised letters, feeling both pride and a knot of anxiety. The boardroom was a place of legends—where the company’s fate was decided, where stories of loyalty and sacrifice were born, and, Kareem suspected, where the real "deal no one understood" (Chapter 2) had been orchestrated. This was the inner sanctum, far removed from "the last day in the plant" (Chapter 1) and its raw emotions.

He dressed carefully that morning, choosing a tie his wife had given him for his first promotion. The elevator ride to the top floor was silent, save for the soft hum of machinery and the distant echo of voices. Security was tighter than usual; new faces in dark suits checked badges and eyed him with polite suspicion. It felt like the prelude to "a new CEO, a new era" (Chapter 4), an era that would likely bring "the layoff letter" (Chapter 5) to many.

The boardroom itself was a study in contrasts. Walnut paneling and sepia photographs of founders lined the walls, but a sleek digital display dominated the far end of the table. The American flag stood in one corner, its colors muted by the morning light. Around the table, the old guard sat stiffly, their faces drawn. Interspersed among them were newcomers—sharp-eyed, impeccably dressed, speaking in clipped, confident tones about "goodwill" (Chapter 7) and "valuation" as if they were abstract concepts, not things that could lead to "dirt in the water" (Chapter 6) or turn a "country on fire" (Chapter 9).

The meeting began with pleasantries, but the air was brittle. Kareem was introduced as a technical advisor, his expertise in systems and integration—and perhaps, unknowingly, his management of "the backup" (Chapter 8) data—cited as vital for the company’s future. He nodded, feeling the weight of expectation and the chill of being an outsider in a room where alliances were forged and broken in whispers, and where the contents of "the private equity file" (Chapter 10) likely dictated every move.

Then the CFO took the floor. He was younger than most, with a voice that carried easily and eyes that missed nothing. His presentation was a masterclass in persuasion: charts, projections, and a vision of global reach. He spoke of efficiency, of unlocking value, of the need to "modernize or perish." The words were smooth, but the subtext was clear—change was coming, and it would not be gentle. This was the man who would soon orchestrate the great "impairment" (Chapter 13), Kareem thought, a man whose decisions would prompt "letters from home" (Chapter 12) filled with despair.

Questions were raised, some sharp, some perfunctory. The old CEO, a fixture for decades, tried to defend the company’s traditions, but his words sounded tired. The vote, when it came, was swift. Hands raised, eyes averted. The CEO was thanked for his service. Kareem watched as the man gathered his papers, dignity intact but future uncertain.

The CFO was named interim CEO. Applause was polite, measured. Kareem felt the room tilt, as if the floor had shifted beneath him. He caught the CFO’s eye—a brief, unreadable glance. In that moment, Kareem understood: the company he had known was gone, replaced by something colder, more efficient, and infinitely more dangerous. This was the beginning of a chain of events that would lead to a critical "meeting" (Chapter 11) and ultimately force Kareem into an "act of will" (Chapter 14).
