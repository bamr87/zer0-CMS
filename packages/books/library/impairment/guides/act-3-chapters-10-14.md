# Act III — Revelation and Reckoning (chapters 10–14)

> **Before you use this guide:** check [`../notes/continuity.md`](../notes/continuity.md) for open questions and [`../notes/characters.md`](../notes/characters.md) for names. Several facts this guide assumes are still undecided.


## Summary
- Conclude the story by revealing the hidden agenda, clarifying the links between the acquisition and the exported equipment, building moral conflict for Kareem, and delivering a meaningful resolution (expose, disappear, or document).

## Group goals
- Produce a clear sequence of escalating revelations that connect prior discoveries (backups, consultant names, investor memos) with a larger pattern of exploitation.
- Provide believable action: meetings, private documents, legal/financial transcriptions, and human collateral (letters, images) that raise stakes.
- Resolve Kareem’s moral arc: he must choose an ending that feels earned (sacrifice, safety, or a public revelation) and maintain credible consequences.

## Per-chapter guidance
- ch10: The Private Equity File
  - Purpose: Reveal the missing link—a private equity file or copy of a consultant’s dealbook that connects the CFO and major players to the acquisition and cover ups.
  - Scenes: Kareem acquires or reads the file (maybe from a contact or a leaked document). Make it a physical file or an email thread that included a valuation model.
  - Hook: Confirmed tie between the acquisition and the export where the vendor, asset flow, and consultant fees create a suspicious loop.

- ch11: The Meeting
  - Purpose: Put critical players face to face (a confrontation, a quiet meeting with an ally—journalist, old colleague, or a board member who doubts the CFO).
  - Scenes: a formal meeting or an underground exchange; use dialogue to reveal motives; the CFO is smooth but defensive. Have a board member or finance lead drop a key phrase.
  - Hook: An explicit moral demand or warning (e.g., someone hints that ‘we’re all on the same ledger’), and a new threat to Kareem’s family safety is implied.

- ch12: Letters from Home
  - Purpose: Human consequences—letters, photos, friends and neighbours in Kareem’s homeland suffer; this chapter ties abstract accounting to real-world harm.
  - Scenes: a letter describing water contamination, a funeral, or the failure of crops. The voice should be intimate and heartbreaking.
  - Hook: A plea to Kareem to help or a direct request for his silence to protect a family member.

- ch13: Impairment
  - Purpose: Public consequence—company announces a massive impairment; market panic. This chapter shows corporate legal/speech vs. the private reality.
  - Scenes: press release drafts, a quiet investor call (mention the stock tumble), and corporate talk vs. people (engineers & laid-off workers) suffering.
  - Hook: a leaked memo or a confirmation that the problem is not a mistake—it’s intentional; the CFO’s web of consultancies is exposed.

- ch14: Act of Will
  - Purpose: Decision & resolution—Kareem chooses a path. This should feel grounded and believable rather than melodramatic.
  - Options to present (choose one based on tone set by previous chapters):
    * Expose: leak documents to journalists or regulators—show aftermath: resignations, arrests, or further cover-ups.
    * Disappear: move to protect the people he loves and start a grassroots cleanup effort; leave a record or online exposé.
    * Document: publish a literary, anonymous account that catalyzes a small, meaningful change.
  - Scenes: final choices, an epilog where the consequences play out, and a highlight of what change occurred (if any).

## Tone & pacing
- Keep the momentum steady: Act III must be reveal-heavy but also character-rich—show the cost of choices.
- Let factual revelations be minimally technical and legally plausible—people use committees, memos, and consultant notes rather than elaborate conspiracies.

## Fact & name continuity
- Ensure the consultant names, the partner company, the CFO name, and the dates match across Act II and Act III.
- If you introduce legal or regulatory agencies (e.g., SEC, local equivalent), be consistent with jurisdiction and scope.

## PR & editorial guidance
- Branch name example: `chapter/ch13-impairment`.
- Commit message: `ch14: add decision & epilogue`.
- PR body: Summarize the chosen resolution and list any earlier chapters that require new references to the final move (e.g., if the expose involve journalist Jane Doe, add her mention earlier in ch8 or ch11).

## Emotional closure & realism
- Avoid tidy endings; instead, aim for realistic reckonings—some people are held to account, while others slip away.
- Conclude the main theme: “goodwill impairment” should mean more than accounting; it becomes the label for moral and human impairment.
