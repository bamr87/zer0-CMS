## Chapter Body

The flight back from his homeland was a blur of recycled air and fragmented memories. Kareem (the protagonist, established in Ch. 1) barely registered the landing. The images from the village—the rust-colored river from `ch6.md`, the children’s vacant eyes, his old colleague’s weary resignation—were seared into his mind. The "dirt in the water" was not just an environmental crisis; it was a personal indictment. The layoff letter (Ch. 5), the boardroom coup (Ch. 3), the deal no one understood (Ch. 2)—it all felt like a prelude to this sickening revelation. His pride in the company’s legacy (Ch. 1) had curdled into a profound sense of guilt.

Sleep offered no escape. Back in his small apartment, the silence was deafening, broken only by the hum of the city that now felt alien. He couldn’t shake the feeling of complicity. The equipment he helped export, the vague specs he’d questioned but ultimately pushed through (Ch. 2), were now instruments of ruin. He had to understand how this happened, how the company he’d dedicated his life to could be responsible for such devastation, a devastation that mirrored the "country on fire" (Ch. 9) he'd read about.

Driven by a desperate need for answers, Kareem opened his old work laptop, a relic from his past life at the company. He navigated to the archived files he’d meticulously backed up over the years—a habit born from a systems engineer’s caution, now a lifeline. He started with the acquisition files, the deal that had led to the new CEO (Ch. 4) and the subsequent "impairment" (Ch. 13). The numbers, once just abstract figures in presentations, now seemed to mock him. Valuations shifted, consultant names appeared and vanished like ghosts in the machine.

He remembered a conversation with a friend from his university days, now a sharp financial analyst working in the city. He messaged her, his fingers fumbling on the keyboard: "Sarah, I need your eyes on something. It’s about the acquisition… and what came after." He attached a few heavily redacted files, his heart pounding.

Hours later, they were on an encrypted call. Sarah’s voice was grim. "Kareem, this valuation… it’s aggressive. Optimistic to the point of fantasy. And these consultants who signed off on it? Their firm has a history. Let’s just say they specialize in making problems disappear, or rather, reappear on someone else’s balance sheet."

Flashbacks assaulted Kareem: the CFO’s slick presentation in the boardroom (Ch. 3), the dismissive waves of hands when he’d raised concerns about the rushed timeline of the international order (Ch. 2). He recalled the pressure to approve technical specifications that were deliberately vague, the celebratory dinner where the talk was all of "goodwill" and "synergy," terms that now tasted like ash. The "private equity file" (Ch. 10) his friend had once warned him about seemed to be materializing from the digital ether.

Sarah’s voice cut through his thoughts. "The goodwill write-down wasn’t a surprise to them, Kareem. It looks like it was part of the plan. They bought a lemon, knew it was a lemon, and structured the deal so they could strip what they wanted and then declare a massive loss. Classic."

The environmental disaster in his homeland, the layoffs, his own shattered career—it wasn’t just incompetence or bad luck. It was a calculated move. The "goodwill" wasn't an asset; it was a void, a black hole created to swallow accountability.

As dawn approached, painting the city skyline in hues of grey, Kareem felt a cold dread solidify within him. He had been more than a pawn; his expertise, his signature on those documents, had been used to legitimize a catastrophe. The realization was a crushing weight, but beneath it, a flicker of resolve. He had to find more. The answers, he suspected, lay buried deeper in "the backup" (Ch. 8) he’d so diligently maintained, a digital trail that could expose the full extent of the deception. His next step was clear, even if the path was fraught with peril. He had to look, not just for himself, but for the people whose lives had been irrevocably altered by the numbers on a ledger. This investigation would be his "act of will" (Ch. 14).
